Scientific data often comes with structure we already know. A molecule has the same energy however it is rotated, a flow behaves the same wherever the origin is placed, and a graph does not depend on the order its nodes are listed. Here I build that structure into the model, mainly through equivariant graph neural networks, for NMR chemical shifts of carbohydrates {% cite bankestad2024carbohydrate %} and crystals, molecular properties {% cite broberg2022pretraining %} and fluid simulation {% cite bankestad2024flexible %}. Graphs themselves are the subject of the Ising model that learns which subgraphs a task needs {% cite bankestad2025ising %}, and of graph networks for matrix factorization {% cite sjolund2022graph %}.
